Prompt 1. Frame challenge (about 0:15 to 0:25)
PURPOSE: We are an MBA consulting team advising Synvitta Diagnostics'
leadership on which U.S. opportunity to pursue first. We want our problem
statement challenged before we build an issue tree on it.

TASK AND SCOPE: Critique the problem statement below. Do not recommend an
opportunity and do not rewrite our statement.

EVIDENCE: Use only the attached case and our draft.
Our statement: [paste]
The five tests: (1) a question that calls for action; (2) names the
decision-maker; (3) defines success and time; (4) states boundaries and
constraints; (5) assumes no untested cause or solution.

OUTPUT AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA: A table with one row per test: pass, fail,
or unclear; the exact words that cause any failure; one question we should
answer to fix it. Then two alternative framings that change what Synvitta's
first U.S. move is for, one sentence each, neither naming a product.

MISSING INFORMATION: If the case does not settle a point, label it "not in
the case" and list it as a question for Synvitta. Do not invent facts about
Synvitta, its products, customers, or regulatory status.

Prompt 2. Tree critique (about 0:25-0:45)
PURPOSE: We are deciding which of three Synvitta opportunities to pursue
first in the U.S. Our decision tree must divide the work cleanly among
teammates before research starts.

TASK AND SCOPE: Critique the structure of our tree. Do not fill in branches
with answers or rank the options.

EVIDENCE: The attached case and our tree: [paste, including how we defined
each criterion]. The case limits the options to SynELISA research kits,
SynArray newborn screening, and SynArray custom panels, and names four
criteria: business potential, readiness, barriers, and speed to first
revenue.

OUTPUT AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA: A list of specific problems, each tagged as
overlap, gap, mixed cuts on one level, or an action posing as a branch,
with the branch it affects and one fix. Flag any fact (for example, FDA
status) that could be counted under two criteria. Then name one alternative
first cut we did not use and what it would reveal. Keep it under 250 words.

MISSING INFORMATION: If a criterion is undefined in our tree, say so rather
than assuming a definition. Do not add options the case excludes.
Prompt 3. Hypothesis attack (about 0:50-0:75)
PURPOSE: Before we spend research time, we want to know whether our
hypotheses are testable and whether our kill conditions are observable.

TASK AND SCOPE: Evaluate each hypothesis below. Do not rewrite them as
settled conclusions and do not recommend an opportunity.

EVIDENCE: The attached case, our problem statement [paste], and our
hypotheses [paste]. Each should have four parts: (1) a specific claim with
a mechanism; (2) evidence that puts it first; (3) why it matters to
Synvitta's decision; (4) an observable result that would prove it wrong.

OUTPUT AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA: For each hypothesis: which of the four
parts is weakest; whether the kill condition is observable in the next
three hours, in 90 days, or neither; one comparison that would best
separate it from a plausible rival explanation. Distinguish an actual
defect from an open question, and say what holds up.

MISSING INFORMATION: Do not invent facts about Synvitta or the U.S. market.
Mark any evidence you suggest that we have not supplied as "to verify."
Prompt 4. Regulatory question list (about 2:00)
PURPOSE: Our recommendation must respect what Synvitta cannot claim without
FDA authorization. We need the right questions for an expert, not legal
advice.

TASK AND SCOPE: List the regulatory and business facts we must verify
before acting on our recommendation. Frame each as a question. Do not
answer them as legal conclusions.

EVIDENCE: The attached case and our draft recommendation: [paste: option,
customer or partner type, business model]. Consider FDA RUO rules, CLIA,
and laboratory-developed tests (LDTs).

OUTPUT AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA: A table: question; why it matters to our
recommendation; who could answer it (for example, regulatory counsel, a
CLIA laboratory director, Synvitta); what we will assume until it is
answered. At most eight rows. For any rule you describe, cite an official
source (FDA or CMS) with its date, or mark it "unverified."

MISSING INFORMATION: Flag anything that may have changed since 2024,
including the status of FDA's LDT rule. If you are not certain a rule
still applies, say so. Do not invent citations.
Prompt 5. Prospect check (about 2:15; optional)
PURPOSE: Our deliverable names 2 to 3 U.S. prospects for Synvitta. Each
must be a real organization that plausibly fits our recommendation.

TASK AND SCOPE: Check the prospects below. Do not add new prospects.

EVIDENCE: Our recommendation [paste one line] and our prospects [paste
names]. Use current web sources.

OUTPUT AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA: For each prospect: does it exist; what it
does that is relevant to our recommendation; the source URL and its date;
fit rated strong, partial, or weak, with one reason. If two sources
disagree, show both.

MISSING INFORMATION: If you cannot find a source, say "not found." Never
infer what an organization buys or does without a source.
Note for students: if you want candidate prospects, ask for them in a separate prompt and treat every name as a lead to verify with this check.

Prompt 6. Skeptical client (about 2:55)
PURPOSE: We present to Synvitta's leadership shortly and want the hardest
questions before they ask them.

PERSPECTIVE (optional): Take the view of Synvitta's CEO: limited cash, no
FDA clinical authorization, and a team that has only sold in Brazil.

TASK AND SCOPE: Challenge our recommendation and 90-day plan. Do not offer
an alternative recommendation.

EVIDENCE: The attached case and our recommendation, 90-day plan, metrics,
and risk: [paste].

OUTPUT AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA: The three hardest questions, each with one
sentence on why it matters; then the single biggest risk we have
understated and what would show it is happening within 90 days. Check
that our plan contains three actions, 2 to 3 named prospects, and two
metrics, and flag anything missing.

MISSING INFORMATION: Base the challenge on the case and our materials. If
a question depends on a fact not supplied, say what fact.
