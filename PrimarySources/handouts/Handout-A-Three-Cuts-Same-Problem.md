## Page 1 — Three ways to cut the first level

The first cut of your issue tree changes the story it tells. Before you commit to one, sketch two
or three and ask what each one makes visible and what it hides. Make your first-pass comparison of
all three opportunities during the first hour (**by 1:00**).

| Cut | Level 1 branches | What it makes visible | What it can hide |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: By opportunity** | SynELISA kits, SynArray newborn screening, SynArray custom panels | A direct comparison on the case’s criteria | That several opportunities may share the same customer |
| **B: By customer type** | Research labs, pharma or CROs, CLIA labs, diagnostic developers, state newborn screening programs | Who can buy now, and who can only partner | Differences in product readiness |
| **C: By business model** | Product sales, services or co-development, partnership or evaluation | How and when cash arrives | Which product carries the model |

**Hard rule (Handout A):** Product and customer type form a **matrix**, not one tree level. Put one
dimension at level 1 and the other at level 2, or draw the matrix. **Never** put a product and a
customer type side by side on the same level.

### MECE check before you research

- **Mutually exclusive:** Could one fact sit in two branches? If so, redefine the branches.
- **Collectively exhaustive:** Do the branches cover the whole parent? The three opportunities are
  exhaustive only because the case sets that boundary. State what you left out (e.g. licensing or
  distributors).
- **One logic per level:** If you cannot say what logic split the parent, the split is probably not MECE.
- **Branches are options, not actions:** “Hire a sales rep” belongs in a work plan, not an issue tree.
